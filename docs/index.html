Which Pages Should a Human Review First? A Rule vs a Model, Tested on Held-Out Clients

Md. Imdadul Haque Somik · Observational study, one sampled month (March 2026) · Decision-support, non-production
Abstract. Editors have limited weekly time, so which pages should they check first for a click-through-rate (CTR) fix? We build a transparent rule (CTR below the norm for the page's search position) and ask whether a gradient-boosting model ranks pages better, using the first 21 days of March 2026 as inputs and the last 10 days as the outcome. On 68,868 pages from 38 held-out clients, the model's observed AUC was 0.91 against 0.79 for the rule (better in 5 of 5 folds), but its top-5% queue captured 37% of later missed clicks against 39% for the rule, and impressions alone reached AUC 0.84. We therefore use the model as a secondary signal and turn the ranking into an action playbook with human review on every row. Results are observed and directional; they do not show that any rewrite recovers clicks.

1Introduction

Decision. A content or SEO specialist with about an hour a week must choose which pages to review: rewrite a title, refresh content, improve links, or leave alone. A false alarm wastes editor time; a miss leaves clicks unclaimed.

Question. Can a model rank pages by later missed clicks better than a simple, explainable rule, on clients it has never seen, and is the gain large enough to justify the complexity? The lane began as a broad "refresh opportunity" score (Week 2) and was narrowed to the CTR-fix queue once the data contract (Week 3) showed which signals were reliably available.

2Data

Release and tables. The FlyRank ML Internship warehouse: a daily page-performance fact table (monthly files, January 2025 to June 2026) joined to a content dimension table (word count, search volume, competition). Rows are anonymised; only hashed identifiers appear.

Window. March 2026 only (9,841,378 daily rows; grain date × client × page, verified unique). The last sample month was avoided because its outcome window may be incomplete. Features use days 1–21; the label uses days 22–31.

Kept and excluded. Kept rows where Search Console data is available (36.7% of rows). Excluded paid-channel columns (driven by ad budgets), AI-referral columns (measured after the fact), same-day outcome columns (too close to the label), and client identifiers as features. Pages need at least 100 impressions and average position 1–20 in days 1–21: 68,868 of 162,301 pages with impressions (42.4%), holding 76% of impressions, across 38 clients. Content fields are 27–34% missing and carry missing-value flags.

3Methodology

Baseline (the rule). Score = max(expected CTR at the page's position band − actual CTR, 0) × impressions, an estimate of missed clicks. Expected CTR is the pooled CTR of eligible pages in the same position band.

Label. Later missed clicks = max(expected CTR × days 22–31 impressions − clicks, 0), with expected CTR frozen from days 1–21. A page is positive when this is in the top quarter of training pages (and at least 1 click): "still a real prize". It is a proxy, not a measured result of a rewrite.

Features (16). Impressions, CTR, average position, active days, one-day spike share, position wobble, last-7-day impression share and CTR, search volume, word and character counts, competition level, and missing flags. Models: impressions-only floor, logistic regression, shallow random forest, small gradient boosting.

Validation. Five-fold GroupKFold by client, so every test client is unseen; identical folds for every method; thresholds learned on training pages only.

Leakage checks. Name and window guards; single-feature scan with a random-noise control (noise AUC 0.509); a deliberate label-derived trap (AUC 0.905 → 1.000, showing the audit would notice a leak); no duplicate pages or shared clients across train/test; and the pooled-norm check (baseline AUC 0.785 pooled vs 0.786 train-only). All checks passed in this sample, which does not prove leakage is absent.

Mean over 5 held-out client folds. Label base rate: 25.4% overall (12.7% to 34.5% by fold), so precision at top 5% should be read against that, and AUC is the fairer discrimination number; AUC fold standard deviations 0.003–0.017. The best model was picked on the same folds it is scored on, so its edge is slightly optimistic.

Reading. The model ranks the whole list better, but the top of the queue, where an editor actually works, is about as good under the plain rule. Impressions alone give much of the AUC, so part of the signal is simply page size (impressions carry most of the permutation importance, 0.33 AUC drop). The model's queue overlaps 73–85% with the rule's. A naive random split would have shown AUC 0.915 vs 0.906 grouped, a small difference here. When tested as a combined ranking (rule × model probability), AUC rose from 0.797 to 0.820 with captured clicks level (41.4% vs 41.3%), so the combined ranking is used.

Persistence check. Pages flagged by the rule stayed below the norm in the later window (85.5% of 41,624 flagged pages; 100% of the top 20), though some regression to the mean is expected.

Do fading pages keep fading? Among 8,131 pages whose impressions were fading in the last observed week, the next 10 days ran at 1.56× the last-7-day rate: they rebounded.

5Limitations & honest framing

One month, one label definition, Search Console pages only; observed and directional. The label is a CTR-gap proxy against a pooled in-sample norm, so it describes "typical this month", not "achievable". Page-level data cannot see query intent: branded queries, answer boxes and AI answers can make low CTR normal. Average position hides daily swings. The data is observational: nothing here shows that a rewrite, refresh or link change recovers clicks, and no click gain should be promised. The CTR-by-position pattern is only partly monotonic (verdict MIXED), and overall CTRs are very low, so the norm should be re-checked on other months. Effort hours below are assumptions for the editorial team to replace.

6Ranked recommendations (action playbook)

Each page gets one reason code, checked in order: data-quality holds first, then actions. Of 68,868 eligible pages, 33,160 (48.2%) fall in the ACT list, 19,858 (28.8%) on HOLD, and 15,850 (23.0%) on MONITOR.

Priorities. (1) Start with page-1 title and description reviews, the cheapest per click at stake, after checking the live results for branded queries or answer boxes. (2) Verify fading pages before refreshing, since many rebound. (3) Work down the list in rank order: 3,367 pages hold half the value, while one 25-page week touches 2.7%, so capacity is the real constraint. (4) Run a small before/after pilot against untouched similar pages before claiming any effect.

Guardrails. Every row requires human review and none is safe to automate. Never auto-publish, delete, redirect or de-index from a score; never judge writers or clients with it. Monitoring: re-score monthly; re-learn the CTR norm if any band moves more than 25%; drop the model ranking if its AUC gain falls below 0.02; revisit the rules if reviewers reject more than 30% of ACT pages.

7Reproducibility

All work is in the project repository under work/notebooks/: data contract, baseline, model, validation audit, action playbook, and capstone. Metric receipts (no data rows) are in work/outputs/*.json. Fixed seeds (42), identical folds for every method. Data access requires the internship's credentials, so the row-level queue is not committed.
